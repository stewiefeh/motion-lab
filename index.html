Motion Lab

A living study of web motion. Everything on this page is the demo: now powered by real libraries: a rigid-body physics pit, a live GLSL shader field, a FLIP-shuffled deck, an inertial flick rail, a constellation sky and a pinned type argument, beside the hand-built originals.

Generative morph

An organic form redrawn every frame. The outline is generated from layered sine fields, so the shape never repeats and never sits still.

This is not a looping video. The path data is regenerated about sixty times a second from a radial noise function, the same trick behind liquid heroes on studio sites.

Flow field

Seven hundred particles riding an invisible current. Drag your pointer through the panel to bend the field; the trails are painted by fading the canvas instead of clearing it.

Shader field

A fragment shader painting every pixel from fractal noise, live on the GPU. Move inside the panel to bend the current; keep scrolling and the field floods with yellow.

Sticky stage

Velocity read

Scroll speed is measured every frame and fed back into the type across this page, so fast flicks skew the headlines and slow ones settle them.

Release

The strip runs out, the pin releases, and the page resumes its vertical story where it left off.

Spring physics

Real integration, not a CSS easing curve. Grab the disc and throw it. Position and velocity are stepped through a damped spring every frame, so it snaps home with an honest wobble.

Pull me

Throw hard and it overshoots; set it down gently and it barely moves. That asymmetry is what separates physics from tweening.

Physics pit

A real rigid-body engine, not a tween. Grab anything and throw it; collisions, gravity and restitution are all simulated, never faked.

Liquid type

A noise field pushing every glyph's pixels. Hover the word to raise the tide; underneath, the turbulence never stops breathing. The text stays selectable the whole time.

Sliced reveal

One artwork, cut into columns. Each column enters on its own beat and resets when you scroll away, so the reveal replays every visit.

Clip-path plus a stagger. No images, no libraries: the artwork is one gradient shared across ten windows.
