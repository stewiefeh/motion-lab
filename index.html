Motion Lab

A living study of web motion. Everything on this page is the demo: variable axes, morphs, flow fields, scroll reels, springs, goo, liquid type, sliced reveals, image trails, a cursor gallery, 3D tilt, ink fill and decoders, each built by hand.

Axis play

One variable typeface, two live axes. Move your pointer across the big word and weight follows distance; the second word stretches as it enters the viewport, driven by the scroll itself.

Generative morph

An organic form redrawn every frame. The outline is generated from layered sine fields, so the shape never repeats and never sits still.

This is not a looping video. The path data is regenerated about sixty times a second from a radial noise function, the same trick behind liquid heroes on studio sites.

Flow field

Seven hundred particles riding an invisible current. Drag your pointer through the panel to bend the field; the trails are painted by fading the canvas instead of clearing it.

Pin, hold, translate

The page stops here and hands your scroll to a horizontal strip. Vertical movement becomes sideways travel, one gesture, two directions.

Sticky stage

A tall section, a sticky viewport and a measured translation. The math is three lines; the feel is pure studio.

Velocity read

Scroll speed is measured every frame and fed back into the type across this page, so fast flicks skew the headlines and slow ones settle them.

Release

The strip runs out, the pin releases, and the page resumes its vertical story where it left off.

Spring physics

Real integration, not a CSS easing curve. Grab the disc and throw it. Position and velocity are stepped through a damped spring every frame, so it snaps home with an honest wobble.

Pull me

Throw hard and it overshoots; set it down gently and it barely moves. That asymmetry is what separates physics from tweening.

Gooey merge

A blur, a hard contrast cut, and blobs become liquid. Move inside the panel: satellites chase the main body and merge where they meet.

Liquid type

A noise field pushing every glyph's pixels. Hover the word to raise the tide; underneath, the turbulence never stops breathing. The text stays selectable the whole time.

MELT

Built from two filter primitives the browser has shipped for years: turbulence generates the noise, the displacement map spends it.

Sliced reveal

One artwork, cut into columns. Each column enters on its own beat and resets when you scroll away, so the reveal replays every visit.

Clip-path plus a stagger. No images, no libraries: the artwork is one gradient shared across ten windows.

Image trail

The award-site hero effect, rebuilt. Move your pointer through the panel and tiles spawn along your path, pop in, then burn off. Fast swipes leave denser trails.

Cursor gallery

A list that carries its own preview. Glide down the rows: the panel chases your pointer with lag and tilt, swapping its gradient for each row it passes.

Depth tilt

One card, three planes. Move around the card and each layer drifts at its own depth while a glare tracks your pointer across the surface.

steady

The headline floats closest to you, the body sits mid-air, the badge hovers above everything. All of it is CSS perspective, no WebGL.

Ink fill

An outlined word that drinks its color as it crosses the viewport, poured by the scroll itself. Scroll back up and it empties again.

SCROLLSCROLL

A clip-path wipe riding the view timeline. Zero JavaScript in browsers that support scroll-driven CSS.

Decoder

Text that resolves itself. Hover each line and the glyphs cycle through noise before locking, left to right, like a signal finding its frequency.
